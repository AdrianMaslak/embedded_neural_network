On screen

4 · SNN design, encoding and simulation evidence

On screen

Diagram 4, then the SNN C-simulation log (1200/1200 bit-exact) and its synthesis report.

Say
  • Encoding: Sobel edges, spike if above a third of the strongest edge, two direction channels, 16×16 grid over the shape's bounding square = 512 inputs.
  • The lecture encoding got 31 %: noise and 45° edges broke it.
  • Network 512–64–32–4 LIF, T = 1 time step, int16 weights; spikes make the multiplications into additions.
  • C-simulation matches the Python integer model bit for bit on all 1200 images.

5 · CPU vs. FPGA split, memory, Vivado

On screen

Diagram 1, then Vivado: block design and Address Editor. Briefly main.c in Vitis (run_cnn, run_snn).

Say
  • CPU: UART, CRC, preprocessing, starts the IP and polls ap_done, measures time.
  • FPGA: only the two networks.
  • CNN reads its image from DDR through HP0 (CPU flushes the cache first). SNN reads its features from an 8 KB AXI BRAM.
  • Bring-up bug worth telling: Vivado left the SNN memory port unconnected, so it hung; fixed by joining the CPU interconnect on S01.
  • Timing met at 100 MHz, 29 % LUT, 58 % BRAM.

8 · Performance comparison

On screen

Report Table 1, then diagram 6 (timing bar).

Say
  • Accuracy 100 % vs 97.9 %; per class the SNN loses 2–3 %.
  • Accelerator 2.84 ms vs 0.197 ms; energy 755 µJ vs 11 µJ.

9 · Limitations, trade-offs, next steps

On screen

Report discussion paragraph, or one closing slide.

Say
  • SNN encoder runs on the CPU (1.8 ms, 9× the SNN itself); moving it to the FPGA is the next step.
  • UART is the bottleneck: Ethernet, sending 1 KB of features instead of 4.8 KB of pixels, DMA with double buffering.
  • CNN C-simulation only ran 400 of 1200 images (PC memory); the board result on all 1200 covers it.
  • DFX bonus not done; T > 1 time steps could trade SNN speed for accuracy.
